## Decision 1: How to review Alton's form without touching it

- **Options:**
  - (a) Submit a test response.
  - (b) Read the public page's form definition (`FB_PUBLIC_LOAD_DATA_`), then render pre-filled links in a real browser.
- **Chosen: (b).**
- **Rejected:** (a) would put a fake entry in Alton's sheet.
- **What (b) gave:**
  - every question, option, required flag and field ID;
  - a confirmed lack of sign-in and of an email field;
  - proof that the app's link selects the option, as Alton's own link does.

## Decision 2: What the link may carry

- **Chosen:** the reader's topic, plus "app details": the version, the language and the prices' `scraped_at`.
- **Why:** these let Alton match a "price looks wrong" report to the exact data the reader saw.
- **Not sent:** anything about the reader. Rating and comments stay the reader's own input in the form.

## Mistakes caught mid-session

| Thought | Caught when | Corrected to | Lesson |
|---------|-------------|--------------|--------|
| "The pre-fill doesn't select the option": the server-rendered HTML showed every option `aria-checked="false"` | Rendering the same link in Chrome showed the option selected; Google applies pre-fill in the browser | A browser check for both the app's link and Alton's | Judge a web page's behaviour by rendering it, not by its raw HTML. |
| The preview check "found" the Feedback button but clicked nothing | The screenshot showed the page unscrolled: Flutter lists off-screen widgets in its accessibility tree | Scroll until the node is inside the viewport, then click | When driving Flutter through its accessibility tree, check the node is on screen. |
